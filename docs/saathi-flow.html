Saathi — intake, dashboard, outputs

What goes in, what the team looks at, what comes out — and which parts of the sketch the code does not do yet.

SystemSaathi v2 · Palanadu Drawn fromserver/index.js · public/ Revision2026-08-09

Saathi already runs the left half of this drawing well: many kinds of paper, voice and photo come in through five intake pages, an AI pass turns each one into structured fields, a staff member corrects it, and it lands in one searchable corpus. The right half — the outputs the office actually wants to hand to the MP — is where the thin parts are. Three intake sources in the sketch have no way in at all, and two of the outputs have nothing behind them.

Built and running Partly there — shape exists, substance doesn't In the sketch, not in the code

Sheet 1 — the whole flow

Nine ways in, one corpus, three pillars, seven ways out

Everything funnels through the same five-stage spine, whatever format it arrived in. The stage that matters most is the third one: no AI extraction reaches the register without a person editing it first, which is why the register can be trusted as a record rather than as a transcript.

Sheet 2 — intake

Six live channels, three missing ones

The live six all accept mixed media in a single submission and merge it into one record rather than one record per attachment — that is the design decision that makes paper-heavy field intake workable. The three missing ones are the sketch's political-content sources, and none of them has a table today.

Sheet 3 — dashboard

Three pillars over one geography

The pillars are already the right shape: Personal is the day, Political is the standing, Governance is the delivery. Two of them are honest today. The third — Political — is mostly illustrative placeholder tiles on the admin page, and it will stay that way until speeches, press meets and scheme data have a way in, because there is nothing real to compute an insight from.

What each pillar reads, and what it's missing

I · Personal. Today's schedule with contacts auto-matched by mandal, a per-contact brief the PA can edit, standing notes carried forward, and speech points for event types where the MP actually addresses a room. This is the most complete pillar. The gap is small: leader summaries exist only where a staff member typed one — nothing synthesises a person's history into a line before a meeting.

II · Political. Live news tagged by mandal, campaign and cluster reports from the field, and how often each contact has been represented. The Constituency Health Score, Voter Sentiment Map, Media Sentiment and Opposition Activity tiles are labelled placeholders — real numbers there depend on intake that does not exist.

III · Governance. The grievance register filtered by mandal, village, category, status and channel; a coverage heatmap showing where grievances cluster; duplicate linking; and department routing per category. Scheme mapping — the arrear / settled / pending columns in the sketch — is the one real hole.

Sheet 4 — the grievance loop

One grievance feeds five surfaces and comes back empty

You asked whether the governance dashboard should sync with grievance tracking. It already does, in one direction — a single grievance record is the atom behind the register, the heatmap, the department letter and the TTD letter. What is missing is the return path: nothing captures what the department actually did, so resolution status is whatever a staff member last typed.

Sheet 6 — to close the sketch

The shortest path from here to the whole drawing

Ordered by what unblocks the most downstream. The first three are intake or schema; the outputs mostly fall out once the data behind them exists, because the pipeline, the filters and the PDF machinery are already built.

  1. Grievance outcome rowsA child table recording who a grievance went to, what came back and when. Closes the loop in Sheet 4 and turns resolution status from a manual field into a derived one.
  2. Scheme master + per-village statusA scheme list with applied / sanctioned / pending / settled counts per village. Without it the Governance pillar's scheme mapping and the development snapshot are both unbuildable.
  3. Speech & press-meet intakeOne more intake page on the existing spine: upload a PDF, Word file or audio, extract the text, tag date, venue, subject and constituency. It reuses the whole pipeline — this is a form, not new infrastructure.
  4. Village as a first-class filter everywhereConstituency and mandal filter well; village is inconsistent across pages. Every dashboard widget and every export should take the same three-level scope.
  5. Retire the placeholder tilesOnce speeches, press meets and scheme data land, replace the Political pillar's illustrative tiles with computed ones — or remove them. A mock number on an MP-facing screen is worse than an empty panel.
  6. Generators for the remaining outputsDevelopment snapshot and tone snapshot, plus a period rollup for campaign reports and draft captions for the social calendar. All of them reuse the brief-PDF machinery and the filter axis.
