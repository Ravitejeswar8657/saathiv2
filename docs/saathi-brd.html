1Business Objective

1.1The business problem

A parliamentary office serving a rural constituency receives citizen requests in the forms its citizens can actually produce — a handwritten paper form pushed across a desk, a telephone call in Telugu, a photograph of a sanction letter, a voice note from a village. It receives political information the same way: a field worker's spoken report, a photographed banner, a newspaper cutting, a scribbled note.

Before Saathi, none of this became information the office could act on collectively. Each intake stream lived in a separate register, a separate spreadsheet, or a staff member's memory. This produced five recurring operational failures:

  • Requests were lost or duplicated. The same citizen approaching the office twice through two different channels generated two unconnected cases, and neither was recognised as a repeat.
  • Prioritisation was personality-driven. Which case was urgent depended on who last spoke to whom, not on any consistent standard the office could defend.
  • Preparation for engagements was manual and inconsistent. Assembling who the Member of Parliament would meet, what they had asked for previously, and what had been promised took hours per day and was frequently incomplete.
  • Field political reporting never reached the leadership. Campaign activity, scheme delivery and cluster-level observations were reported informally and never aggregated.
  • Nothing was measurable. The office could not state which villages it had neglected, which categories of request dominated a mandal, or whether a commitment had been honoured.

1.2Value delivered

Saathi converts an unstructured, multi-channel, multi-lingual inflow into a single verified operational record, and turns that record into the specific outputs the office must produce each day.

Table 1.1 — Value by business dimension

Citizen servicePaper forms filed manually; no way to tell whether a citizen had approached before.Every request captured in whatever form it arrived, recognised against existing cases, and tracked to a resolution status.
ConsistencyUrgency assigned informally, case by case.A fixed, published prioritisation standard applied identically to every request, and explainable to anyone who questions the ordering.

1.3Guiding business principles

Three principles govern the design of every capability in this document and should govern every future change to it.

Principle 1 — Assisted, never autonomous

Automated extraction produces a proposal. A member of staff reviews and corrects that proposal, and the corrected version — not the machine's reading — becomes the official record. No automated output is ever committed to the register unseen.

Principle 2 — Advice is separated from action

Where the system suggests a reply to a citizen, a next step, or the wording of a letter to a government department, that suggestion is written to advisory fields only. It can never populate the fields that record what the office actually did. The audit trail records decisions taken by people.

Principle 3 — The source is never discarded

The original photograph, scanned document or voice recording is retained against the case permanently, so any staff member can re-verify a doubtful reading against the original at any point in the future.

1.4Proposed success measures

  • Elapsed time from a citizen presenting a request to that request being visible in the register, with a category and a priority.
  • Percentage of automatically extracted fields corrected by staff at review — the direct measure of extraction reliability.
  • Percentage of repeat approaches correctly identified as linked to an existing case.
  • Proportion of open requests older than their recorded deadline.
  • Number of villages with registered stakeholders that have received no engagement within the neglect threshold.
  • Preparation time per engagement, measured before and after adoption.

3.1Capability A — Constituent Request Capture

Purpose. To accept a citizen request in whatever form the citizen and the office can produce it, without forcing a member of staff to transcribe it first.

Business trigger. A citizen presents at the office in person, or telephones it. Alternatively, a member of staff processes a batch of paper forms collected earlier in the day.

Termination. The capability ends when a verified request is committed to the register, or when the member of staff discards the draft.

Table 3.1 — Business rules: constituent request capture
Ref.RuleBusiness rationale and behaviourState
GRV-01Recognised intake channels are In-Person Walk-In and Telephone Call.These are the two channels through which the office genuinely receives citizens. Historic records carry two retired messaging channels; those values are preserved for continuity but no new request may be created against them.In place
GRV-02A submission must contain at least one of: typed notes, a photograph or scanned document, or a voice recording.An entirely empty submission is rejected and the officer is told, in plain terms, that at least one of the three is required. Any combination of the three is permitted.In place
GRV-03A single submission produces exactly one request record, regardless of how many attachments it carries.A citizen presenting a form, a supporting photograph and a spoken explanation has made one request, not three. This rule prevents the register being inflated by attachment count.In place
GRV-04Bulk capture permits up to 20 photographed forms in one batch; each form becomes its own separate request.Supports the end-of-day processing of a stack of paper forms collected at a camp or the front desk. Here each form genuinely is a distinct citizen, so the one-record rule applies per form.In place
GRV-05A single submission permits up to 10 photographs or documents and one voice recording, each up to 15 MB.Sufficient for a multi-page form with supporting evidence and a spoken explanation, while protecting the service from oversized uploads on poor rural connections.In place
GRV-06Accepted formats are standard photograph formats and PDF documents for imagery, and the common voice-recording formats for audio.An unsupported file is rejected immediately, naming the file type, so the officer can convert or re-capture rather than discovering the failure later.In place
GRV-07Attachments are held provisionally until the request is committed.Discarding a draft releases its attachments. Any attachment left unclaimed for 24 hours is cleared automatically, so an abandoned review does not accumulate citizen material indefinitely.In place
GRV-08If the automated extraction service is unavailable, capture is refused with a clear explanation rather than failing silently.The register itself remains fully usable for direct manual entry, so an outage degrades the service but never stops the office recording a citizen.In place

3.2Capability B — Assisted Extraction and Mandatory Verification

Purpose. To convert captured material into structured request information, and to guarantee that a human being confirms it before it becomes an official record.

Business trigger. Material is captured under Capability A.

Table 3.2 — Business rules: extraction and verification
Ref.RuleBusiness rationale and behaviourState
GRV-09No extracted request may be saved without staff review.Every extraction is presented in an editable preview. The officer may correct any field. The corrected version becomes the record. This is the single most important control in the platform.In place

GRV-11Where several sources are present, precedence is fixed: the photographed form governs identity and location; the spoken account governs the nature and urgency of the request.Reflects reality — a form carries the citizen's written name, village and telephone number; the spoken account carries the explanation of what is actually wrong.In place

GRV-13Any detail typed by the officer overrides the corresponding extracted value outright.Staff knowledge of a citizen or a village is authoritative over an automated reading.In place
GRV-14Where extraction of one attachment fails, the submission continues and the failure is recorded in place against that attachment.One unreadable photograph must not discard a citizen's entire submission. The officer sees which part failed and supplies it manually.In place

3.3Capability C — Duplicate Identification and Case Linking

Purpose. To recognise when a citizen has approached the office before, so that the office responds to a person's history rather than to an isolated request.

Business trigger. Runs continuously as an officer types into the capture form, and again automatically whenever a request is built.

Table 3.3 — Business rules: duplicate identification
Ref.RuleBusiness rationale and behaviourState
GRV-18An exact match on telephone number is treated as a confirmed relationship and the two requests are linked automatically in both directions.A telephone number is a reliable identifier in this context. Linking both ways means the connection is visible from either request, whichever the officer opens first.In place
GRV-19A similar request description yields up to five possible matches. These are advisory and are not linked automatically.Similar wording is a hint, not proof. The officer decides. Comparison is only attempted where the description is substantive enough to be meaningful.In place
GRV-20A matching name and village in combination yields a possible match, advisory only.Names recur across a constituency; name alone is not a signal. Name together with village is worth showing an officer, but not worth acting on automatically.In place
GRV-21Possible matches are displayed live during data entry, before the request is committed.The officer can ask the citizen "have you been to us before about this?" while the citizen is still present — the only moment at which the question can be resolved cheaply.In place
GRV-22Linked requests display a visible indicator in the register.Anyone reviewing a case sees immediately that it is part of a repeat approach and can review the history before responding.In place

3.4Capability D — Request Classification and Prioritisation

Purpose. To apply one consistent, explainable standard of importance across every request, independent of who captured it.

Business trigger. Classification is proposed at extraction; the priority is calculated at the moment the request is committed and recalculated on every subsequent amendment to category or urgency.

Table 3.4 — Business rules: classification and prioritisation
Ref.RuleBusiness rationale and behaviourState
GRV-23Every request is assigned exactly one category from a fixed, published list of 25 request categories.A closed list is what makes reporting comparable across mandals and across months. The full list, with departmental ownership, is at Appendix A.In place
GRV-24Every category carries a fixed institutional weight between 30 and 90, set by the office as a matter of policy.Expresses the office's standing position on relative importance — medical assistance and law and order rank highest; organisational and courtesy matters rank lowest.In place
GRV-25Every request carries an urgency of High, Medium or Low, with Medium as the default where none can be determined.Urgency is a judgement about this particular citizen's situation, proposed automatically and confirmed or corrected by the officer, with a stated reason.In place
GRV-26Priority is calculated as 40 per cent category weight and 60 per cent urgency.Deliberately weights the individual circumstance above the category. A low-ranking category presented as genuinely urgent will outrank a high-ranking category presented as routine.In place
GRV-27The calculation is fixed and identical for every request, and the resulting priority is stored with the request.The office must be able to explain to any citizen, official or member why one case ranked above another. Storing the result means historic ordering remains explainable even after the policy weights change.In place
GRV-28Amending category or urgency immediately recalculates the priority.Prevents a corrected classification from leaving a stale priority behind it.In place

3.6Capability F — Temple Reference Letter Issuance

Purpose. To issue temple darshan reference letters as a controlled outcome of a citizen request, under fixed entitlement limits.

Business trigger. A citizen request of this nature exists and a member of staff raises a letter from it.

Scope note

Reference letters are not a separate intake channel. They are raised from an existing citizen request and are managed within the request register, where the office already works. This document therefore treats them as an outcome of Capability E, not as an independent capability.

Table 3.6 — Business rules: reference letter issuance
Ref.RuleBusiness rationale and behaviourState
GRV-38A letter requires a date, a recipient name and a darshan type from a fixed list of two.An incomplete letter is refused. The two recognised types are Break Darshan and Supadam.In place
GRV-39Party size is capped by darshan type: Break Darshan — 6 persons; Supadam — 10 persons.These are the entitlements the office may extend. A request above the cap is refused, stating the type, the limit and the number requested.In place
GRV-40Party size, where stated, must be a whole number of at least one.Basic integrity check preventing a letter being issued for a fractional or zero party.In place
GRV-41Prior letters for the same national identity number are surfaced as a warning at the point of issue.Alerts staff to repeat beneficiaries so the office can apply judgement. It is a warning, not a block — repeat issue may be entirely legitimate.In place
GRV-42Letters raised directly are Confirmed. GRV-43An issued letter remains linked to the originating request.The register shows what was granted to whom and on what basis, from the citizen's case rather than from a separate list.In place

4End-to-End Business Workflow

Four business journeys are described. Each states the successful path first, then the exception paths as they present to the user.

4.1Journey 1 — Citizen request, capture to resolution

Successful path

  1. Presentation. A citizen attends the office with a completed paper form, or telephones to describe a problem.
  2. Capture. The Front Office Officer records the approach in whatever form is available — photographing the form, typing a summary of the call, recording the citizen's own spoken account, or any combination. Nothing is transcribed at this stage.
  3. Duplicate check. As the officer enters the citizen's details, the system surfaces earlier approaches. A telephone match is presented as confirmed; a similar description or a matching name and village is presented as a possibility for the officer to test with the citizen while they are still present.
  4. Proposal. The system reads the captured material and proposes a complete request: the citizen's identity and location, the nature of the request, its category and its urgency, with the reasoning for the urgency stated.
  5. Verification. The officer reviews the proposal in an editable form, corrects anything misread, and confirms it. This step cannot be bypassed.
  6. Commitment. On confirmation the request enters the register. Its priority is calculated from its category and urgency. Any confirmed duplicate is linked in both directions. The original photographs and recordings are retained against the case.
  7. Progression. A staff member assigns an officer, sets a deadline where appropriate, and records the action taken. Status moves from Pending to In Progress.
  8. Referral. Where a government department must act, staff request a draft letter. The system addresses it to the department and officer mapped to the request's category and produces a draft, which staff edit and then issue as a formal document under the Member's name.
  9. Closure. When the matter is settled, status is set to Resolved. The request leaves the open count and remains permanently searchable.

Exception paths

ConditionWhat the user experiences
Nothing captured — no notes, no attachment, no recordingThe submission is refused with a plain instruction that at least one of the three is required. Nothing is recorded.
An unsupported file type is attachedThe submission is refused immediately, naming the offending file type, so the officer can re-capture rather than discovering the problem after the citizen has left.
One of several attachments cannot be readCapture continues. The failure is shown against that specific attachment, and the officer supplies the missing detail manually from the retained original.
The automated reading is wrongThe officer corrects it in the review form. The correction becomes the record; the machine's reading is discarded.
The submission is not a request at allIt is flagged. The officer either discards it or retains it as Feedback, which is counted separately and never inflates the open-request position.
The officer abandons the reviewThe draft is discarded and its attachments released. Anything left unclaimed is cleared automatically after 24 hours.
Automated extraction is unavailableAssisted capture is refused with a clear explanation. The officer records the request manually; no citizen is turned away.
A formal letter is requested before one has been draftedThe request is refused with an instruction to draft the letter first, preventing an empty document being issued under the Member's name.

4.2Journey 2 — Engagement, from scheduling to briefing

Successful path

  1. Scheduling. The Personal Assistant records the engagement with its name, date and mandal, and may classify its type and tag the audience communities it addresses.
  2. Automatic stakeholder identification. The system attaches the stakeholders registered in that mandal, ordered by standing, each carrying their history with the office and any open request.
  3. Stakeholder approval. The Assistant reviews the proposed list, writes or adjusts a briefing note for each individual, and approves the list. Preparation cannot proceed until this approval is given.
  4. Speaking points. Where the engagement type is one at which the Member addresses a room, a draft set of speaking points is offered, built around the village, the local leaders present, the outstanding requests in that area and the day's local news. The Assistant edits it. For other engagement types this stage is skipped, though the Assistant may open it deliberately.
  5. Personal touches. The Assistant records the informal elements that make an engagement land locally.
  6. News. The Assistant selects the coverage the Member should be aware of, with items relating to the day's mandals presented first.
  7. Issue. The Assistant previews and produces the briefing document for the date, rendered so that Telugu content is correct, and provides it to the Member.

Exception paths

ConditionWhat the user experiences
The engagement lacks a name, date or mandalIt is refused. Without a mandal no stakeholders can be identified and the engagement cannot be prepared for.
The Assistant attempts to advance before approving the stakeholder listProgression is blocked. This is the workflow's one hard gate and exists so that no briefing is issued without a person having confirmed who the Member will meet.
The engagement type does not warrant a speechThe stage is omitted automatically. The Assistant may open it if the occasion warrants it after all.
No engagements exist for the requested dateThe Assistant is told plainly that the date holds no engagements, distinguishing an empty day from a failure.
No local news is availablePreparation continues without it. Absent news never blocks the briefing.

4.3Journey 3 — Political field report

Successful path

  1. Observation. A Field Political Coordinator observes something worth reporting — a campaign event held, a scheme reaching or failing to reach a village, a cluster-level development.
  2. Capture. The coordinator submits typed notes, photographs of what they saw, a spoken account, or any combination — the same capture standard as a citizen request, requiring no additional training.
  3. Proposal. The system proposes a single structured report: a title, the report type, the delivery status, the mandal and village, and a description assembled from every source supplied.
  4. Verification. The report is reviewed and corrected before it is committed, exactly as a citizen request is.
  5. Consolidation. The report joins the field reporting register, filterable by type, delivery status and mandal, and remains editable as the situation develops.

Exception paths and current limitation

ConditionWhat the user experiences
Nothing captured, or an unsupported file attachedRefused with the same plain messages as citizen request capture.
The situation changes after reportingThe coordinator amends the report and updates its delivery status — for example from Ongoing to Delayed.
The report reaches the leadership viewIt does not. Reports are captured, classified and located, but no leadership summary reads them; the corresponding executive indicators remain illustrative. This is requirement POL-08 and is the largest single gap between what the office collects and what it sees.

4.4Journey 4 — Governance oversight and the open loop

Current path

  1. Aggregation. Committed requests aggregate automatically by category, status, mandal and village.
  2. Geographic review. The Chief of Staff reviews concentration and dominant category by mandal, and reviews which places with registered stakeholders have received no engagement within the neglect threshold.
  3. Direction. Escalation is directed at concentrations of unresolved requests, and engagements are directed at neglected areas.
  4. Referral. Formal correspondence is issued to the responsible department.
  5. Manual closure. A staff member who learns of an outcome updates the request's status by hand.
Business risk — the loop is open

Step 5 is the weakness in the process. Nothing captures the department's response, so the recorded status reflects only what a staff member remembered to update. Three consequences follow directly, and all three are recorded as Proposed requirements in Section 3:

  • The office cannot measure departmental responsiveness, and so cannot escalate on evidence.
  • The office cannot produce a development delivery position by village, which is its most-requested leadership output.
  • Resolution figures cannot be relied upon for external reporting, because they measure record-keeping diligence rather than resolution.
